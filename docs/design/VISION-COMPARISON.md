# Creator workspace versus daily briefing

Prepared: **21 October 2026**. Status: **assessment and recommendations, not approved scope changes**. Inputs: the founder's [reference image](founder-reference-2026-10-21.png), the [existing visual concept](../demos/product-preview/index.html), SPEC v1.0 and the founder's original idea/settled answers. The reference is a design input, not evidence that its depicted data, freshness or features work.

## Recommendation

Use the founder's creator-led workspace as the product's visual/navigation foundation. Keep the connected daily briefing prominent and the default initial reading view. The stronger combined product is a personal place to understand chosen creators over time: daily synthesis for catching up, creator/update browsing for exploring, and a useful history for returning.

This is a concrete recommendation, not a claim that either design has demonstrated higher retention, demand or payment. The existing demo emphasized completing a briefing and underrepresented attachment to particular creators and exploratory use. The founder's image makes those needs clearer. It does not automatically cancel the accepted five-minute daily recap, chosen-time delivery, dated history or explicit-interest rules.

## Better understanding of the reader

Working interpretation: **people who deliberately follow trusted people to stay informed, improve at something or make decisions, and want their attention to belong to them across platforms.** They may want both a short answer and the ability to inspect a favorite creator. This is an interpretation of the reference, not validated persona research.

Keep the three founder-selected audience hypotheses separate:

- **Consultants and other professional readers:** prepare for conversations or decisions, see meaningful developments, distinguish disagreement from repetition and retrieve supporting sources. The briefing supplies speed; creator/topic history supplies credibility and depth.
- **Individual marketing/brand-monitoring buyers:** examine chosen creators, brand mentions and explicitly disclosed sponsors. Creator/brand drill-downs have a stronger role. This does not establish demand for campaign measurement, live monitoring or a whole team product.
- **General readers with serious interests:** recognize their chosen people, understand what matters, choose worthwhile originals and return to saved ideas. Hobbies and personal interests should work as well as career topics. Willingness to pay remains unknown.

The professional-development creators in this reference suggest a useful test context, not permission to exclude the other audiences. Teams would add a distinct buyer/workflow: shared ownership, permissions, annotations and reporting. Neither image validates those needs.

## Recommended information structure

- **Left: people and editable collections.** A creator can group the public platform channels the reader explicitly chose. Following one channel must not silently subscribe the reader to every discovered account; identity matching must be clear.
- **Center: Today’s recap / All updates.** Keep up to five connected topics and dated history easy to reach. All updates keeps creator cards, latest-first ordering and format filters as exploration. A date/range control need not occupy the creator rail.
- **Right: meaningful signals.** Show supported new developments, shared references, brand mentions and disclosed sponsors with evidence. Counts explain coverage; they do not establish importance, independent corroboration or audience reach.

Useful illustrative signals: several chosen creators discuss the same new development; two link the same study; a source adds a material counterpoint; a sponsorship is explicitly disclosed. Every signal needs its source set and time window. Repetition and a shared underlying reference should be visible rather than mistaken for independent confirmation.

Improve the individual card: show actual takeaways and context, preserve creator identity and original link, and expand for evidence. Use imagery when it helps identify or understand the original. A large generic decorative thumbnail should not displace the useful findings.

Do not borrow “Live feed” as a commitment without a separate decision. The agreed service checks daily and produces one local-time recap. Show a truthful last-check/cutoff label. Publication timestamps can be recent without implying real-time collection. Threads appears in the reference but is not an approved eighth source family. Search, bookmarks and read-later controls are useful proposals, not already completed features. Begin with one clear saved destination if bookmarking is added.

## Priority proposals for return use

1. **Creator continuity across chosen channels.** Make “What has this person said recently?” easy to answer, with platform identity and citations. This is the clearest missing part of the existing demo. It is a navigation proposal, not permission to expand collection automatically.
2. **What changed since you last caught up.** Distinguish new information, continuing themes, disagreement and corrections. Link earlier dated context. Returning after a missed day should reduce work, not punish readers with unread-count debt. Automated cross-day comparison is additional work to scope/evaluate, not a proven existing capability.
3. **Find and reuse saved insight.** A minimal saved library and history search can answer “Who linked that study last week?” Retain source/reference attribution. Start with straightforward retrieval; broad AI questioning and project workspaces are later possibilities, not necessary launch assumptions.

The existing accepted daily time, short recap, quiet-day honesty and completion state already support a repeatable habit. Additional reminders, streaks and time spent scrolling are not substitutes for useful briefings. Measure useful returns, successful retrieval and actual renewal, separately by audience.

## Two plausible distribution loops

**Editable source collections:** a reader deliberately shares “The people I follow for product thinking.” A recipient previews creator/source links, chooses which to copy and edits their own collection. Keep copies independent by default and make any later curator updates explicit. This adapts an existing documented sharing pattern: Reader offers public curated-document bundles and an import action, but those are documents, not automatic creator-feed subscriptions. [Reader sharing](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/sharing)

**Small cited insight cards:** share one useful comparison or finding, with date, creator credit and original links. Let the recipient get value before signup, then choose relevant sources to follow. Require an explicit preview of what becomes public; personal interests, full private recaps and full original content are not shared automatically. Reader documents public annotated links and highlight images; this supports the interaction pattern, not an outcome claim. [Reader sharing](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/sharing)

For collections, measure shared-link visit → source adoption → first useful recap → retained reader. For insight cards, measure useful recipient engagement → activation → continued use. Shares and signups alone do not establish viral growth. Postpone paid referral incentives until service economics and referred-user retention are measured.

Private project shelves can support retention and professional reuse. They are not automatically viral; inviting a teammate does not by itself establish a network effect. Feedly distinguishes personal saved-topic boards from private team collaboration/public dissemination. [Personal boards](https://docs.feedly.com/article/715-what-is-a-personal-board), [board sharing](https://docs.feedly.com/article/61-how-can-i-share-a-board)

## Current competitive reality

Summaries, creator/source aggregation, citations and brand/entity mentions do not alone establish differentiation. Readless documents thematic synthesis of chosen newsletters/Substack/RSS with source references. Inoreader documents article summaries, entity mentions and scheduled cross-article reports. These are public feature claims, not hands-on quality findings. [Readless Synthesis](https://www.readless.app/features/ai-synthesis), [Inoreader Intelligence](https://www.inoreader.com/features/intelligence)

The product hypothesis to test is that the combined creator-first experience and dependable longitudinal understanding are sufficiently better for a particular audience's work/interests. Broad access and source interpretation still need actual proof when D08 is reopened. No documented competitor feature proves that therecap will retain users or grow rapidly. Old Inoreader Bundles announcements should not be cited as current support: its official notice says that feature was deprecated in 2024. [Official notice](https://www.inoreader.com/uk/blog/2014/10/bundles-collect-share-and-discover-best.html)
